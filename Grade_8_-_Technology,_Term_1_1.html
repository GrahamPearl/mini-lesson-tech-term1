    Term 1 Reference

    Key Facts & Structural Principles

    Structure Type & Purpose

    An electrical pylon is a frame structure designed to support heavy electrical cables safely over a great distance.

    Triangulation (Member C)

    Forming triangular shapes with structural members braces the frame so it becomes rigid and stable—triangles resist forces without changing shape.

    Cross-Bracing (Member B)

    Members crossing one another stiffen the structure, distribute bidirectional wind loads, and increase the maximum cable load the pylon can support.

    First Electrical Pylon

    Built on 14 July 1928 at Bonnyfield, Scotland to carry Britain's early national grid transmission lines.

    Community & Visual Impact

    Visual impact is the visual influence an object has on its immediate surroundings. Community concerns include visual pollution, children climbing and falling, loose objects falling onto passers-by, and perceived cancer risks.
