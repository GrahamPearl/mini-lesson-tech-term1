CAPS Technology Reference

Forces, Triangulation & Structural Failure

Struts & Compression (Red)

A strut is a rigid structural member that resists a pushing, squashing force called compression. Examples include vertical water tower legs, bridge top chords, and roof truss rafters. Long, slender struts fail by buckling unless shortened with triangulation.

Ties & Tension (Blue)

A tie is a member that holds or pulls two parts of a structure together under a stretching force called tension. Examples include bridge bottom chords, roof tie beams, king posts, and pylon guy cables. Excessive tension causes a member to stretch and fracture.

Triangulation & Cross-Bracing

Triangles resist deformation because their side lengths lock their angles rigid. Cross-bracing stiffens rectangular bays by crossing two diagonal members in an X shape, stopping lateral wind from shearing a tower sideways.

Four Structural Failure Modes

Buckling: ends bend inward under compression. Flexing: unbraced beams or joints curve outward under bending load. Fracture: material snaps when stress exceeds strength. Toppling over: a narrow base or top-heavy load lets the Centre of Gravity move outside the support footprint.
